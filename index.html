Two ways a plant gets into your garden

"Sowing" just means planting a seed. The only real question is where the seed sprouts and grows up: straight in your garden bed, or in a little pot first and then moved out. The chart uses two shades of green for exactly this — here's what they mean.

Direct sow

Dark green bar · "plant out"

You put the seed straight into the garden bed where it will live its whole life. You poke it into the soil at the depth on the seed packet (a rough rule: about twice as deep as the seed is wide — tiny seeds barely covered, big seeds an inch down), water gently, and wait for it to sprout right there.

Keep the top inch of soil lightly damp every day until the seedlings come up — baby seeds dry out and die fast. Once they're a few inches tall, you switch to normal deep watering and thin them (pull the weakest so the rest have room).

Best for: plants that hate having their roots disturbed — carrots, beets, radishes, beans, cucumbers, squash, melons, zinnias, cilantro. On the chart these have only a dark-green bar.

Start seeds, then transplant

Light green bar → dark green bar

You sprout the seed in a small pot or seed tray first (indoors or in a sheltered shady spot), give it 6–8 weeks to grow into a sturdy baby plant, then dig a hole in the bed and move the whole little plant out — roots, soil, and all. Moving it is called transplanting.

Why bother? It gives slow crops a head start while it's still too hot or cold outside, and a strong young plant survives the garden better than a fragile sprout. Honest shortcut: you can skip this entirely and just buy a "transplant" — a young plant already started for you — at any nursery. That's what your store-bought tomato and jalapeño are.

Best for: slow starters and fussy heat/cold timing — tomatoes, peppers, eggplant, broccoli, cauliflower, cabbage. On the chart the light-green bar is the pot stage, the dark-green bar is when it goes in the ground.

Sow — to plant a seed. "Direct sow" = plant it right in the garden.
Transplant — to move a young plant from a pot into the ground (or the plant itself).
Seedling — a baby plant, just sprouted and still small.
Thinning — pulling out extra crowded seedlings so survivors have space.
Hardening off — letting a pot-grown plant sit outside a few hours a day for a week before transplanting, so the move doesn't shock it.
Slip / start / transplant (to buy) — a young plant someone else already grew, ready to put in your bed.
Shade cloth — loose-weave fabric (usually 30–40%) draped over hoops to cut harsh sun. Each crop's card below has a colored Shade line saying whether and when it needs it.
